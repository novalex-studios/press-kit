# Press Kit

A turn-based narrative survival voyage, 1904–05. Pile coal on the decks and you'll reach Dakar even if the colliers don't come. But the ships get slower and the men suffer for it. Decisions like this fill 224 days, from the Baltic to Tsushima.

**The battle at the end is already lost in the history books. The seven months before it are yours.**

In 1904 Russia sent its Baltic warships halfway round the world to fight Japan. You command that squadron for 224 days and thirteen legs, from the Baltic to the Strait of Tsushima. Before each leg you read the chart, the coal figure and the mood of the crews. Then you set one standing order and put to sea. The weather, the neutral ports and your own crews answer, and the next leg starts from whatever is left.

## The battle is decided on the way

There's no tactical combat. Tsushima is an authored set-piece with a handful of admiral's decisions, and the squadron you bring decides it. You win or lose it at the coaling stops and anchorages long before the strait.

## How it can end

St Petersburg can relieve you of command before you reach the strait. If you lose the battle, the squadron dies there. If you survive the defeat, you face captivity or a court-martial. The verdict counts the capital ships you brought through.

## A decision, for example

Pile coal on the decks and the squadron can reach Dakar even if the colliers miss the rendezvous. But a deck load costs speed and stability, and the men pay for it in morale and wear every day it stays aboard. Sail lean and the ships stay handy, but now everything rides on the colliers turning up.

## One standing order for each leg

Press On or Keep Station. Exercise the Guns or Husband the Crews. Run Dark or Steady Passage. The order you pick holds until the next leg. Coal, morale, gunnery training, hull fouling and cohesion pull on one another, so a slip in one tends to drag the others down. For the worst days you have a few last resorts, such as a vodka ration or news kept from the lower decks. Each one works once, and then it's gone for the voyage.

Every voyage has a number. Give a friend yours, and they get the same weather, breakdowns and events.

## Choices that change the route

At Tangier, you can send the smaller, shallower-draught ships through Suez or keep the whole squadron together round the Cape. At Madagascar, you can wait for Nebogatov's reinforcements or sail without them. At the end, you choose your strait: Korea Strait, La Pérouse or Tsugaru.

## The chief engineer's letters

Eugene Politovsky, the squadron's chief engineer, wrote to his wife almost every day. His letters narrate the voyage. They end at sea, shortly before the battle, and he was lost with the flagship Knyaz Suvorov at Tsushima.

## History, and where the game departs from it

The route, the ships, the dates and the disasters are historical: the Dogger Bank night, the long wait off Madagascar, Nebogatov's old ships catching up off Indochina. Every authored event is tagged historical, plausible or counterfactual, and every historical one cites its sources. The ship plates come from period books and photographs, and all of them are public domain and credited in the game.
