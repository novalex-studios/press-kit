# Press Kit

A turn-based narrative survival voyage, 1904–05. Command Russia's Second Pacific Squadron across 224 days to Tsushima: ration coal, keep order, outlast disease and mutiny, and arrive with a squadron worth the name. Sail it better than history did.

**The battle at the end is already lost in the history books. The seven months before it are yours.**

Day by day you sail the voyage from the Baltic to the Strait of Tsushima: thirteen legs, 224 days. Before each leg you read the chart, the coal figure and the mood of the crews, set the one standing order the squadron sails under until the next leg, and put to sea. Then the weather, the neutral ports and your own crews answer, and the next leg starts from whatever is left.

## A decision, for example

Pile coal on the decks and the squadron can reach Dakar even if the colliers miss the rendezvous. But a deck load costs speed and stability, and the men pay for it in morale and wear every day it stays aboard. Sail lean and the ships stay handy, but everything now rides on the colliers turning up.

## What makes it different

- **The journey is the game.** Coal, morale, gunnery training, hull fouling and fleet cohesion pull on one another; when one slips, the rest tend to follow, across all 224 days.
- **The battle is the exam.** There is no tactical combat. Tsushima is an authored set-piece with a handful of admiral's decisions, and the squadron you bring decides it. You win or lose it long before, at the coaling stops and anchorages on the way. St Petersburg can relieve you mid-voyage; lose the battle and the squadron dies in the strait; survive that defeat and you face captivity or court-martial.
- **Real forks.** At Tangier, send the smaller, shallower-draught ships through Suez or keep the whole squadron together round the Cape. At Madagascar, wait for Nebogatov's reinforcements or sail without them. At the end, choose your strait: Korea Strait, La Pérouse or Tsugaru.
- **Your orders shape the squadron.** Press On or Keep Station, Exercise the Guns or Husband the Crews, Run Dark or Steady Passage: the order you pick each leg decides which squadron arrives.
- **The Admiral's Expedients.** A handful of last resorts, each spent once and gone for the voyage: a vodka ration, an example made, news kept from the lower decks. Each buys relief at a price.
- **Every voyage has its order of the day.** The same order-of-the-day number brings the same weather, breakdowns, events and fates. Give a friend yours and they sail the same voyage; only the decisions differ.

## Told by the men who were there

The voyage is narrated from the letters of Eugene Politovsky, the squadron's chief engineer, who wrote to his wife almost every day and was lost with the flagship Knyaz Suvorov at Tsushima, and from Vladimir Semenov, who survived it. Politovsky's letters end at sea, shortly before the battle; Semenov's account carries the story into the strait.

## History, and where the game departs from it

The route, the ships, the dates and the disasters are historical: the Dogger Bank night, the long wait off Madagascar, Nebogatov's old ships catching up off Indochina. Every authored event is tagged historical, plausible or counterfactual, and every historical one cites its sources.

## Real ships, real sources

Recognition silhouettes are traced from period plates and photographs, mostly Jane's; Codex plates come from period books and photographs, among them Jane's, Brassey's Naval Annual and Apostoli's postcards. All are public domain and credited in the game.
